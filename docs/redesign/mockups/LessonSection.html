A small network finds them on its own

Now we hand a neural network the raw x1 and x2 columns. No radius. No angle. No trigonometry. Nothing of what you worked out in Section 4.

We use scikit-learn's MLPClassifier here deliberately, because the point of this section is the result, not the mechanism. Lesson 1.2 opens up the mechanism by hand, and Lesson 1.3 rebuilds it in PyTorch. For now, treat it as a black box that we are about to justify opening.

Why this matters Section 4 needed your knowledge of spirals. This one needs none. Watch for what replaces it.

ΣThe maths, step by stepWhere do 4,417 parameters come from?

The boundary curves. It wraps around the arms, follows the spiral outward, and does so without anyone telling it that spirals have a radius and an angle.

The network's first layer transformed (x1, x2) into 64 new numbers. The second layer transformed those into 64 more. Only the final layer performed the classification, and it is, in essence, a logistic regression, exactly like the one that failed in Section 3.

Glossary Logistic regression A linear classifier that passes a weighted sum of the inputs through a sigmoid to output a probability. Its decision boundary is a straight line. Used in 1.1 · 1.2 · 2.3 See it on the concept map

The network's final layer is essentially the same logistic regression that failed in §3. Why does it now succeed?

Why: the earlier layers are the feature engineering, fitted rather than written. The final straight cut works because it is made in the learned space.

How to use this platform

A short tour of what is on every lesson page, and how the pieces fit together. Nothing needs an account: your progress lives in this browser.

Learn

1

Read a lesson, or take it one section at a time

Each lesson opens with its goal, what you will be able to do by the end, the data it uses and an honest time estimate. The On this page panel tracks where you are. If a long page feels like a lot, press Focus mode at the top of the lesson: you see one section at a time, and the lesson remembers where you stopped.

2

Play with the interactive labs

Boxes marked Interactive are live experiments that run in your browser, placed where the idea they show is explained. Move the sliders, press play, break things on purpose. Some labs carry a challenge: hit the goal and see where your result sits among everyone who has tried, then share it.

3

Run the code, then write some

Every code cell from the notebook is here with the output it actually produced; numbered markers on some cells match line-by-line notes underneath. In the early lessons, cells marked Run run in your browser (the first run loads Python, which takes a few seconds). Press Edit to change a cell and run it again. Exercises ask you to fill in the blanks; Check tests your code, and hints and a solution are there when you need them.

4

Look up any term, and see how the ideas connect

Words with a dotted underline have a definition: hover, or tap on a phone. The glossary lists them all, and the concept map shows what each idea builds on, what builds on it, and where it comes back later in the course.

Practise

5

Answer the checkpoint at the end of each section

A Checkpoint closes every section: a few quick questions with an explanation for each answer, right or wrong. Some ask you to explain an idea in your own words; the tutor reads your answer and tells you what is solid and what is missing. In focus mode, the next section waits until you have answered, and Skip for now is always there.

6

Think before you look

Question cards in the text come in three flavours: Check your understanding, Pause & think and Try it yourself. Press Write my answer to jot down your thinking (kept on this device), then Show explanation to compare it with a worked answer. The quiz at the end of the lesson draws the threads together.

7

Come back for a daily review

Every question you answer joins your daily review. Questions you miss come back tomorrow; ones you get right come back after longer and longer gaps (a week, then two weeks, a month, and so on). A few minutes a day is what turns a lesson you read into one you remember. The number beside Daily review in the sidebar shows how many are due.

Track and share

8

Master each lesson, pass each module

A lesson is mastered when you have read it through, answered most of its checkpoints and got at least 70% right, and finished its quiz with the same score. The panel at the end of each lesson shows what is left; mastered lessons get a gold dot in the sidebar. Each module ends with a module challenge: harder questions that mix ideas from several lessons. {% if features.certificate %}Pass all of them to earn the certificate{% if services.api %}, which carries a link anyone can use to check it is genuine{% endif %}.{% else %}Pass it to earn that module's badge; My progress shows the badges you have.{% endif %}

9

Keep a streak going

My progress shows your streak, a weekly goal you set yourself, a calendar of the days you studied, a map of your skills with the weakest ones first, the badges you have earned and your best lab challenge results.

10

Carry your progress to another device

On My progress, turn on sync to get a private code. Enter it (or open the link it gives you) on your phone or another computer and your answers, notes, streak and badges follow you. There is no account and no email: whoever has the code has the progress, so keep it to yourself. The tutor key, if you entered one, never leaves the browser it was typed into.

Ask, discuss, and run it for real

The Ask the tutor button answers questions about the lesson you are on.{% if discussions %} Under every lesson there is a discussion (a GitHub account is needed to post) for questions other learners may share.{% endif %} Every lesson also has a Download notebook button: open it in Jupyter, VS Code or Google Colab, run it, change it. Reading builds understanding; running the code is where it sticks.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • ⌘ K or /: search
  • Alt + ← →: previous or next lesson (in focus mode: previous or next section)
  • Shift + Enter in a code editor: run the cell
  • Esc: close search, images and pop-ups

The labs work with the keyboard too: Tab to a control, then use the arrow keys to change it.
